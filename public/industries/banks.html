What banks still put in the mail

The core already built the file. Paper is the last mile.

  • Customer notices

    Account, service, and change notices that still require a physical document. You export. We produce.

  • Statement files

    Cycle files the core already generates, plus a list. Recurring jobs use the same path. Statements & invoices →

  • Disclosures

    Multi-page PDF or Word packages. The words stay in your system. Production is the print and postage.

  • Proof mail you add

    Certified Mail or Certificate of Mailing when the notice needs formal proof. You add it. You approve it.

The core generates. A person still says yes.

Cycle mail still leaves the core as PDFs and recipient lists. That export is the handoff we take. We do not generate statements inside banking software. We do not sit in origination. We do not sit in bill-pay. Those systems create the file. This platform prints it.

For a regulated sender, the risk is not a missing printer. The risk is a notice that leaves without a person looking at the setup. Software can propose addresses and print options. A human on the app still confirms. Nothing prints on a silent schedule. That approve click is the control, not a speed bump.

Recurring statement jobs use the same path as a one-off notice. Same upload. Same review. Same person on the hook before production. Ops clicks yes. The core does not. Approve debits a prepaid meter. There are no public unit prices on this site.

Keep cycle statements and exception notices on separate jobs when the files differ. A monthly statement file is not a rate-change disclosure. A closed-account notice is not an invoice. Export what the core already made. Do not ask this platform to assemble the pages inside the core.

Some notices need formal proof. Most cycle statements do not. Add Certified Mail or Certificate of Mailing before you approve when the file needs it. Same job as First-Class. Never same-day. Not a postcard. Certified Mail → Included First-Class tracing is not a substitute for that proof. Tracing →

Addresses get checked on the production path. Fewer returns. Not a delivery-day promise. Address verification → Put the institution mark on the envelope if you want it. Logo stock is included; it still needs approval and can take 1–2 business days. Features → This page does not publish a SOC seal. It does not claim a bank partnership. How we handle accounts, jobs, and recipient data lives on Security. Security →

Exception mail is where ops feels the approve click. A rate change. A closed account. A disclosure legal just signed. Those files still export from the core. They still wait for a person. Cycle statements are the volume. Exception notices are the control you can see. Do not let the core auto-push either one.

Statement cycles can sit on a scheduled mail date. Exception notices often need the next window. Pick the mail date on the job. Cutoffs live on How it works. Do not invent a USPS arrival day for a disclosure. Cutoffs →

Bank mail FAQ

Does USMail.ai mail bank notices and statements?

Yes. Notices, disclosures, and statement or invoice files your core already generates, plus a list if you need one. A person on the app approves. We print, insert, apply postage, and deliver the pieces to USPS. We do not generate those files inside a core banking system.

Do you generate statements inside core banking, origination, or bill-pay?

No. Those systems already make the file. We take the export. We do not sit inside core banking, loan origination, or bill-pay. Recurring statement jobs use the same production path. Statements & invoices →

Why does a person have to approve a bank mailing?

Because regulated senders do not want silent auto-mail. Software can propose addresses and print options. A human on the app still says yes before anything prints. That click is the control. Agents cannot skip it.

Do you claim SOC 2 or a bank partnership on this page?

No. This page does not publish a SOC seal or a bank-partnership claim. How accounts, jobs, and recipient data are handled: Security →
