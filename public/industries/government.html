What cities and counties still put in the mail

Residents still get paper. The department still has to send it.

    General correspondence that still requires a physical document. Upload the file the department already uses.

  • Bills

    Recurring bills and statements your system already makes, plus a list. Statements & invoices →

  • Tax, code, and utility correspondence

    The notices finance, code, and utility desks already generate. We do not write the ordinance language.

A human approves citizen mail. Then you buy the meter.

A clerk can upload a notice and a list. Software can propose setup. Citizen mail still waits for a person on the app. No silent auto-mail of a resident notice, a bill, or a code letter. The department keeps the words. Production starts after someone with authority says yes.

That is the difference between a queue and a mailing. Agenda software runs meetings. Ticket systems run cases. Neither one prints, inserts, applies postage, and delivers the pieces to USPS. This platform does the mail job after the file exists.

How departments buy: open the app. Account plus prepaid meter. Approve debits the meter. No public unit prices. No free production mail. Purchasing does not need a walk-up quote for every resident list. Finance, code, and utility desks can share one account or run their own. The file still belongs to the desk that generated it.

Keep tax correspondence, code notices, and utility bills as separate jobs when the documents differ. A clerk can stage the upload. A supervisor still has to confirm citizen mail. That split is the control. It is not a delay for its own sake.
